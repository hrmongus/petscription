# Applications & Rollout

## Website
A shop that reads like a mill's noticeboard.

- **Home:** the wordmark, one line of the manifesto, and the current batch
  number set at hero scale in Instrument Serif. Product grid below. No carousel,
  no video header, no dog running in slow motion through a field.
- **Product page:** the bag photographed on a real counter; the full ingredient
  deck exposed on the page, not behind a tab; the batch page linked inline.
- **`/b/0412` — the batch page.** The whole brand in one URL. Mill date, run
  size, every farm in that run, and the note from the mill floor. Permanent,
  crawlable, linkable. Print the URL on the bag and honour it for years.
- **Type on screen:** Space Grotesk for text, Instrument Serif reserved for
  headlines and numbers, Space Mono for every spec, price, and button label.
- **Motion:** 120 ms, ease-out, opacity and 4px translate. Nothing bounces.

## Retail
- **Shelf talker:** 60 × 90 mm kraft card, one recipe, 40 words, hairline border.
- **Counter unit:** unfinished ply tray, roundel branded (actual heat brand) into
  the front edge, holding 1 kg bags and tins of The Garnish.
- **Sampling:** 60 g kraft sachet, stamp block only — no recipe name. If someone
  asks what it is, that's the conversation working.

## Social
Three formats, rotating, nothing else:
1. **The batch post.** Photo of the stamp block from this week's run. Caption is
   the run size and one fact.
2. **The dog, undignified.** Asleep on a vent, mid-yawn, wedged behind a door.
   Never a hero shot. Never a bandana.
3. **The mill.** Hands, grain, the machine, the radio. No faces needed.

Never: reels set to trending audio, "tag a friend whose dog…", dog-in-sunglasses,
anything with a countdown sticker.

## Email
Plain-text-feeling HTML: one column, hairline rules, Space Mono headers, no
buttons wider than the text. The batch card lands in the inbox the day the run
is milled. Two sends a month, maximum.

## Vet and breeder channel
Same bag, different sheet: a one-page spec in Space Mono with the full analysis,
kcal/kg, and the cold-press temperature curve. No brand poetry — this audience is
buying the numbers, and giving them the numbers *is* the brand behaving consistently.

# Rollout — first twelve months

| Phase | Months | Ships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Proof** | 0–3 | No. 01 and No. 03 only. 5 kg only. Batch pages live from day one. Direct-to-consumer, one city. |
| **2 · Range** | 3–6 | Add No. 02, 04, 05, 06. Add 1 kg and 12 kg. The Standing Order opens. |
| **3 · Shelf** | 6–9 | Independent grocers and one regional pet chain. Counter unit, shelf talkers, sampling sachets. |
| **4 · Extras** | 9–12 | The Garnish, House Broth, the scoop. First limited run (No. 07 Late Harvest) as the autumn moment. |

**What would prove this is working by month 12:** repeat rate over 55% on The
Standing Order, and unprompted mentions of *batch numbers* — not "the oat one",
but "0412" — in customer email. If people are quoting the batch number back at
us, the paper trail is doing the branding.

**What would kill it:** a supply story we can't name (an unnamed grain broker, a
protein of unclear origin) that we ship anyway. The moment the trail has a gap in
it and we paper over it, there is no brand left — only a nice-looking kraft bag.
