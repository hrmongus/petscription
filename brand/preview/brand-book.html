HOWL & OATS

Cold-pressed kibble · Small batch · Grain-positive

Dinner you could read out loud.

Kibble sold the way good coffee and good bread are sold: milled in runs of 400 kilos, dated on every bag, with the farm named on the front. Not a clinical white bag, not a wolf on a black one — a thing you'd leave out on the counter.

01 The fight

The category only sells two stories, and both of them are tired.

Premium dog food is either a white bag that reads like medication, or a black bag with a mountain on it selling an ancestral fantasy the buyer knows is a fantasy. Nobody is selling dog food by naming the farm, dating the batch, and then shutting up.

The contrarian bet

The category ran to grain-free and stayed there. We run the other way and say so on the front of the bag: named heritage grains, named farms. Rolled oats, hulled barley, whole rye — not "ancient grains," but these grains, from that mill. It gives us the name, the palette, and a fight to pick.

The brand idea

Someone in this relationship should be paying attention. The dog does not care. The dog would eat a shoe. Enormous care aimed at an entirely indifferent audience — that tension is the whole brand, and it's why the tone is dry instead of sentimental.

Four pillars — each one cashed out in something physical

Milled, not manufactured
Runs of 400 kg · cold-pressed at 55 °C

Run size is printed on the bag. Mill day is Tuesday, and we say so.

A paper trail
Batch · mill date · best-by, inked per run

Every batch number resolves to a page listing every farm in that run.

Grain-positive
Oats · barley · rye, credited like a menu

The grain supplier is named on the front of the bag, not the back.

Dry, not cute
No fur babies · no exclamation points

The back-of-bag manifesto is the ad. Nothing else needs to be.

02 Name & voice

A pun that earns its keep.

HOWL & OATS carries the wild half and the pantry half in three syllables, and it commits us to the grain fight before a single line of copy is written. It reads as a partnership — a shop name, not a laboratory. Set it in caps, with the ampersand italic; that ampersand is the only decorated character in the entire system.

We mill in runs of 400 kilos, which is not efficient. We put a date on every bag, which is not required. We name the farm that rolled the oats, which nobody asked us to do. Your dog does not care about any of this. Your dog would eat a shoe. We do it anyway. Someone in this relationship should be paying attention. Back of bag · milled Tuesdays, in Vermont

03 Marks

A moon and a grain. Deliberately, no dog.

The category is full of dog silhouettes and wolf heads. A crescent with an oat sprig rising into it says the same thing with more restraint — and it survives being shrunk, which a dog's face never does. Built on a 200-unit circle: moon at (134, 66) r28, sprig on a 63° axis rising from the lower left into the crescent's hollow.

Clear space & minimums

Clear space is the cap-height of the H on all four sides. Wordmark 120px, roundel 96px, sprig mark 40px, monogram 24px — the monogram is the favicon, because ring text turns to mud below 96px.

Never

No outline, emboss, gradient or drop shadow. No recolour outside the palette. No rotating the roundel to make it playful. No mark inside another shape. And no paw print — ever.

04 Colour

Kraft paper, loden green, and one loud accent held in reserve.

Grounds do the heavy lifting and accents are rationed to roughly one per surface. Every ratio below is computed against Oat Milk, not estimated — which is why Ember ships in two values: the beautiful one for display and spot ink, and a darker one for anything a person actually has to read.

05 Type

Three faces, four jobs, no overlap.

The typographic idea of the system is the last row: the typewriter face is reserved for data that changes from one run to the next. It's the tell that a human inked something for this batch. Spend it on a section heading and it stops meaning anything.

06 The line

Numbered recipes, named for the dog — never the benefit.

A senior dog is an elder statesman. A small dog is small talk. A dog with a bad gut is rummaging around in the understory. The number is how staff and repeat buyers talk; the name is what makes someone pick the bag up. The job is printed plainly underneath, in a label face, and never smuggled into the name.

Each recipe owns one accent, and it is the only accent on that bag. Six kraft bags in six notes read as one system on a shelf of competitors shouting in full colour.

07 The bag

It has to survive being left out on the counter.

A flat-bottom kraft stand-up pouch with a tin tie — a coffee bag built for 5 kg, not a plastic sack with a retriever printed on it. Matte uncoated kraft, two spot inks maximum per SKU, no gradients and no varnish. If a bag needs a third ink to work, the layout is wrong.

The crescent window

The die-cut repeats the mark, shows the actual kibble, and means we can never quietly make the product uglier. 60 mm, registered ±1 mm, matte anti-scuff film behind it.

The stamp block

The most important asset we own: genuinely variable data, inked per run, linking to the batch page. It never gets prettified, shrunk below 7 pt, or moved to the back. It is the proof behind every claim in the strategy.

The rest of the kit follows from the bag: an embossed tin tie, a 40 mm gummed roundel that closes the shipper and ends up on a laptop, a plain carton with the manifesto printed inside the lid, and a batch card in every shipment carrying one paragraph about what went right or wrong at the mill that week.

08 Rollout

Prove the trail before widening the range.

M0–3
Proof
Two recipes · one city

No. 01 and No. 03 only, 5 kg only, direct to consumer. Batch pages live from day one — if the trail isn't real at ten customers it will never be real at ten thousand.

M3–6
Range
Core six · three sizes · subscription

Add 02, 04, 05, 06. Add 1 kg and 12 kg. The Standing Order opens: six weeks by default, cancellable from the card, no retention offer.

M6–9
Shelf
Independents · one regional chain

Counter unit, shelf talkers, and 60 g sampling sachets carrying the stamp block and no recipe name. If someone has to ask what it is, that's the conversation working.

M9–12
Extras
Toppers · broth · the first limited run

The Garnish, House Broth, the stamped steel scoop, and No. 07 Late Harvest as the autumn moment — venison, squash, rye, roughly 2,000 bags, gone when it's gone.

What proves it worked

Repeat rate above 55% on The Standing Order, and customers quoting batch numbers back at us — "0412", not "the oat one". If people are citing the paper trail, the paper trail is doing the branding.

What would kill it

A supply story we can't name — an unnamed grain broker, a protein of unclear origin — that we ship anyway. The moment the trail has a gap and we paper over it, there's no brand left. Only a nice-looking kraft bag.
