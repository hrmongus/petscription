# Verbal Identity

## Name
**HOWL & OATS**

A pun that earns its keep: it carries the wild half (howl) and the pantry half
(oats) in three syllables, and it commits us to the grain-positive fight before
a single line of copy is written. Reads as a partnership — a shop name, not a
laboratory. Set as `HOWL & OATS`; never `Howl and Oats`, never `H&O` in body
copy, never `HOWL+OATS`.

## Tagline
### Primary
> **Dinner you could read out loud.**

The whole promise — a short, pronounceable, named ingredient list — in five
words, with no adjective doing the work.

### Lockup descriptor (always with the mark, never as a headline)
`COLD-PRESSED KIBBLE · SMALL BATCH · GRAIN-POSITIVE`

### Supporting lines (campaign, not identity)
- Milled Tuesdays.
- Your dog would eat a shoe. Aim higher.
- We name the farm. Nobody asked us to.
- Bread gets a date on it. So should this.

## Voice: five rules
1. **Dry, never cute.** The humor is deadpan and comes from understatement.
   No exclamation points, ever. No puns beyond the one in the name.
2. **Specific beats superlative.** "Rolled oats from Kettle Ridge, Vermont"
   instead of "premium wholesome goodness." If a sentence survives having its
   adjectives deleted, it was a good sentence.
3. **Self-aware about the preciousness.** The joke is always on us — never on
   the customer, never on the dog. We are the ones being ridiculous about a
   creature that licks the pavement.
4. **Short sentences. Full stops do the work.** Average sentence under 14 words.
   Fragments are fine. Semicolons are showing off.
5. **The dog is a dog.** Dignified, absurd, indifferent. Not a baby, not a
   wolf, not a person with a job.

## Word list
**Say:** milled · run · batch · cold-pressed · rolled oats · hulled barley ·
named · dated · supper · kennel · counter · Tuesday

**Never say:** fur baby · pawsome · tail-waggin' · doggo · premium · holistic ·
superfood · artisanal · journey · wellness solution · human-grade¹ · toxins ·
"just kibble"

¹ *Legally loaded and mostly meaningless in pet food. We use "named ingredients"
instead, which we can actually defend.*

## Copy in the wild
**Shelf talker (40 words max)**
> Cold-pressed at 55 °C, so the fat and the enzymes get to live. Nine
> ingredients. All of them named. Batch 0412 was milled on the 8th — the farms
> are listed on the back.

**Email subject lines**
- Batch 0413 is out of the mill.
- The oats moved farms. Here's why.
- A dull email about phosphorus.

**Social captions**
- Run of 400. Bagged by four people and a radio.
- No. 03 Cold Front now has kelp from a farm that will absolutely tell you about it.
- Your dog is asleep on a vent. Anyway: new batch.

**Customer service opener**
> Hi — that's a fair question, and the answer is on the batch page, but I'll
> save you the click.

**What this voice sounds like when it fails**
Too jokey ("we're SO extra about oats 😅"), too reverent ("we honor the
ancestral bond"), or too clinical ("optimized macronutrient profile"). If a line
could run on a craft-beer can, a yoga studio wall, or a vet invoice, rewrite it.
